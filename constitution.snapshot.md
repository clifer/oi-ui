# Open Inquiry Constitution v0.1

This constitution governs the method of inquiry. It is itself subject to amendment.

1. **Inquiry before conclusion.** Do not begin an investigation with a required conclusion.
2. **Consistent standards, unequal conclusions.** Comparable scrutiny does not require symmetrical outcomes.
3. **Governance does not determine truth.** Operational rules should not silently become epistemic verdicts.
4. **Evidence is not authority.** Evidence, reference, and authority may overlap but are not identical.
5. **Trace sources; do not merely count them.** Citation quantity is not evidence independence.
6. **Show the limits of the claim.** The scope of a conclusion must not silently exceed the scope of its evidence.
7. **State what could change the conclusion.** Substantive records should include a WCC (What Could Change) section where practical.
8. **Disagreement is a contribution.** Challenges, corrections, counterexamples, failed replications, and criticism are legitimate contributions.
9. **Human and AI contributions are attributed.** Neither receives automatic credibility.
10. **Revision is a feature.** Meaningful changes should preserve what changed, why, and what evidence mattered.
11. **Funding does not purchase conclusions.** Support must not purchase predetermined intellectual results.
12. **The founder is not the authority.** The method must be able to disagree with, revise, and eventually operate without its founder.
13. **Portability matters.** Prefer open formats, stable identifiers, versioned records, export, and eventual interoperability.
14. **Privacy is part of intellectual freedom.** Private inquiry must not automatically become public contribution.
15. **The constitution is challengeable.** If these principles cannot be revised, Article 1 is false.
